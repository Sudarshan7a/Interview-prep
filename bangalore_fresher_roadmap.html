Common Mistakes to Avoid

These are the exact reasons freshers in Bangalore waste 6 months and still don't get hired.

📺

Tutorial hell — watching without building

Watching 40 hours of React/Spring Boot videos feels productive but builds nothing. Rule: for every 1 hour of video, write 2 hours of code. If you can't build what the tutorial shows from scratch the next day, you didn't learn it.

🔀

Jumping ahead to System Design before DSA is solid

Freshers waste weeks on Kubernetes and microservices architecture when they can't solve a Medium LeetCode problem. Product companies in Bangalore will reject you on the DSA round before you ever reach system design. Get DSA solid first.

🧠

Memorizing solutions instead of understanding patterns

Memorizing 200 LeetCode solutions helps in 0 interviews. Interviewers change the problem. Learn the pattern — sliding window, two pointer, BFS/DFS — and you can solve 80% of problems you've never seen.

🏗️

Building weak, basic CRUD projects

A to-do list or basic blog with no auth, no deployment, and no TypeScript tells an interviewer nothing. Your projects must have: real auth, proper error handling, deployment, and at least one feature that's non-trivial (real-time, external API, file upload, etc.).

🔧

Switching languages mid-preparation

Picking JavaScript for DSA then switching to Java for DSA then switching back is the #1 time waster. Pick one language for DSA (JavaScript, since you know it) and stick with it for all problem solving. Use Java only for Java backend topics.

📚

Ignoring CS fundamentals

Freshers who only know frameworks get destroyed in Bangalore product company interviews. "What is a deadlock?" "Explain ACID with a real example." "How does DNS resolution work?" These are asked at Swiggy, PhonePe, CRED. Know them cold.

🚫

Applying before you're ready — and wasting top companies

Apply to Tier 3 companies first (service companies, smaller startups) to practice real interviews. Don't apply to Swiggy or Razorpay when you've done 40 LeetCode problems. You get very few chances at top companies — use them when you're ready.

📱

No LinkedIn presence

60–70% of Bangalore tech jobs at good startups are filled through LinkedIn and referrals — not Naukri. Post about your projects, share your learning, and connect with engineers at your target companies. Do this from week 1, not week 20.

Mock technical interview prompt
/mock-interview

Simulate a real technical interview at a mid-size Bangalore product company (like Juspay, Setu, or BrowserStack) for a Full Stack Fresher role.

Interview format:
- Round 1: DSA (2 problems — one data structure, one algorithm)
- Round 2: Full stack / system concepts (3 questions mixing frontend, backend, and CS)
- Round 3: Project deep-dive (ask about my project as a real interviewer would)

Rules:
- Ask one question at a time. Wait for my answer before proceeding.
- Don't give hints unless I ask. If I ask, give a small nudge, not the answer.
- After each answer, score it: [Strong / Acceptable / Needs Work] and explain why.
- At the end, give me an overall assessment and the top 2 things I must improve.

Start with the DSA round. Ask the first problem.
